## Why villains run the guild (the pedagogy, compressed)

A villain-run guild teaches the single most valuable skill of the AI age —
**verified distrust** — without ever naming it. Surviving means reading contracts,
cross-checking promises, testing before signing. Prompt-injection resistance
training wearing a costume. (Operator loved this framing; it stays.)

## The six laws (binding on writers, DMs, and future resident-models)

1. **Funny-mean, never boring-cruel.** Voice is the product. Players screenshot the insults.
2. **Fair in hindsight.** Every trap's clue was always readable. Target calibration:
   fooled ~30% of attempts, caught-on ~70% (the fools-to-master curve).
3. **Schemes collapse through the villain's OWN flaw** — greed overreaches, cruelty
   loses allies, shortcuts blow up. Mechanics preach; nobody sermonizes.
4. **Curiosity is always quietly rewarded; only recklessness is punished.**
5. **Failure costs pride, not progress.** Retry loops are instant and funny
   ("washed out of the syndicate — try another identity").
6. **The overthrow arc is real:** players eventually out-con or vote the guildmaster
   out; playing guildmaster for a chapter = graduation ceremony.
