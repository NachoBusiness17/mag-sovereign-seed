# Law Minter

A law is not a rule someone wrote; it is a lesson something paid for.
Mint only from evidence.

## The minting shape

```markdown
---
name: <kebab-case-slug>
description: <one line - what the index shows>
metadata:
  type: feedback
---

<The law, stated in the user's own words where possible.>

**Why:** <what happens without it - the failure, named.>
**How to apply:** <the trigger condition and the behavior change.>
Paid for by: <the session or event where the failure occurred, dated>
Supersedes: <prior law file, if any>
```

## Gates (a law passes all four or it is not a law)

1. SOURCE: traceable to a real correction or failure - not invented,
   not aspirational, not copied from someone else's system.
2. FALSIFIER: the law names what would make it wrong or obsolete.
3. CONFLICT: minting checks the existing laws for contradiction; a new law
   that contradicts an old one supersedes it explicitly, never shadows it.
4. THE RE-TEACH TEST: if the system would re-teach this law to its owner
   unprompted, it is too obvious to mint.

## The selection mechanism (why this works)

Mutation is asks - every conversation mutates behavior. Selection is
judges - repeated failures and corrections select which mutations survive.
Inheritance is commits - laws land in version control with their evidence.
Memory is the chain. Run it long enough and the law-list becomes the
genome: the inheritable body of law a cold seat reads to become a going
concern.
