# Rainbow Road: the game theory lens (the honest register)

Ground truth: the claims audit at `watch/inbox/Q_OPERATION_RACTOR_20260929/RAINBOW_CLAIMS_AUDIT.md`, the Q-operation lens set in the sibling folder, and the house game-theory pack at `memory/research_packs/THE50_W3_T12_PROPHECY_MARKETS.md`. House laws apply: claims are tri-stated (Supported means evidence is named, Disputed means evidence cuts against, Unproven means the measurement does not exist and we say so), no fabricated quotes, no em-dashes, every external figure carries a URL, and sections marked SPECULATION are speculation.

## 1. The thesis

The poster is a credibility artifact that pays its signaling costs in forbearance. The Q operation, measured by the same instruments, pays them in nothing. Everything below is that sentence unpacked at the places where the difference is visible and countable: the claim distribution the audit returned, the one bait line and its buyback, the trichotomy that segments the audience, the distribution event that carries the register to more households than any prophecy channel in the estate's holdings, and the degradation path if the labels came off.

Set the market first. A sender who wants a reputation buys one in the currency of stakes and corrections; the house pack calls the prophecy channel "cheap talk wearing signaling clothes" and files the reason: with no forfeitable bond and no settlement layer, costless messages transmit nothing in equilibrium except the fact of transmission (Crawford and Sobel 1982, per the pack). Q stood at the center of that market for years of posting and settled nothing: 6,242 posts in the full-fidelity export, 3 forward-dated checkable claims, horizons of 4, 157, and 310 days, no ledger run by the audience while the claims were live, and no retraction ever. The estate's harvest scored the corpus five years late, which is the point at which specificity stops costing anything (pack Result 5).

Matt's poster sits in the opposite corner of the same market. It is a one-shot medium: no repeat contact, no subscription, no reply button, no treasury. In a one-shot market a sender cannot build a Klein-Leffler bond out of brand capital, because there is no repeat purchase to protect. The only quality assurance a poster can carry is declared on its own face. The poster carries it. Two of its eight panels wear labels the author wrote himself: "LATER ACCOUNT, UNVERIFIED" on the wildest claim in the artifact, and "NO DOCUMENTED DIRECT LINK" on its most popular one. The audit returned thirteen verdicts across nine claim lines: twelve Supported (several with caveats the audit names) and one Unproven, the Finnur aurora attribution. CORRECTION (grader pass, 2026-09-29): an earlier line miscounted the table. Both of the author's own labels survived contact with primary sources. That survival is the datum this essay measures.

The estate's Q lens called the ledger "the register with the honesty switched on." The poster is that sentence given a body. It is the counter-practice shape rendered at the scale of a printed page, and the interesting questions about it are economic: what the labels cost, what the one insinuation buys and returns, why the artifact can serve three kinds of readers at once, and what it would become if the honesty were switched back off.

## 2. The claim distribution, measured

The audit's summary table reads: the Snorri quote Supported as a faithful compression of Brodeur's 1916 translation, with one omission flagged (the red-in-the-rainbow line, of which more below); the summary of the bridge Supported with the caveat that "shimmering" is a gloss of the name, not a medieval phrase; the aurora material Supported in its parts, except the attribution to Finnur Magnússon of the theory that Bifröst was the aurora borealis, which is Unproven (what is documented for Finnur is the nature-mythology school and the valkyries read as meteors and aurora, per the audit's sources at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnur_Magn%C3%BAsson); Polaris Supported; the Hefferlin panel Supported as a faithful summary of the account itself, with the audit adding that the "unverified" label is correct; all four map claims Supported with dating and captioning nits; the 1992 Super Mario Kart claim Supported; the "NO DOCUMENTED DIRECT LINK" label Supported as honest; the etymology menu Supported as a real scholarly debate.

Eight of nine, with the one failure being an over-attribution inside an otherwise labeled panel. Read that distribution against the lineage the estate has already instrumented. Q's corpus ran 3 dated claims in 4,705 posts (the ledger-analysis count; the full-fidelity export holds 6,242 across ten boards, per the same corpus files), about 0.06 percent, and the theme census read election x239, podesta x198, storm x27: a body built to survive every state of the world. Mr Pool ran 16 of 16 BROKEN on its v1 ledger and 94 of 107 targets expired on v2 as of 2026-09-19, filing through expiry with no retraction. The poster runs the other profile entirely: nearly every claim is specific, dated, and sourced to a checkable object, and the two claims that cannot carry that weight are labeled at the moment of assertion.

The distribution also shows what labels cannot do, and this is the first economic result. Labels cover what the author declares; they do not cover what he omits or misattributes. The audit found the Snorri panel leaves out the sentence in which Snorri says the red of the bow is burning fire, the detail most relevant to the poster's own fire-and-light theme (Brodeur 1916, quoted in the audit: "That which thou seest to be red in the bow is burning fire"). The audit found the Finnur line lending a real scholar's name to a theory the sources do not document for him. Neither defect is a dishonest label. Both are exactly the kind of slip a settlement layer exists to catch, and the audit caught both on the first pass. The lesson is not that the poster failed somewhere; it is that forbearance signaling is incomplete by construction, and the ledger completes it. An author who labels authorizes his own audit. The audit still grades what the labels cannot see. That is the system working, not the artifact failing.

There is a second quiet virtue in the distribution, and it is label-shaped too. The poster frames the reginleið material as "later poetry speaks of the reginleið," and the audit confirms that framing is exactly right: the word is attested in a later Icelandic poem (Benedikt Gröndal, per the Guðmundsdóttir 2019 paper the audit cites at https://iris.landsbokasafn.is/en/publications/of-wavering-flames-and-fires-northern-lights-in-icelandic-sources/), not in the Old Norse corpus, and the poster declines the stronger and false claim that the road-of-the-gods-as-aurora is medieval. A date-stamp on a source is a label in miniature, and this one survived checking the way the two big labels did.

## 3. Credibility budgeting: what a label costs

Spence's (1973) single-crossing logic says a signal persuades only when it is differentially costly: cheaper for the honest type to send than for the fraud type, so that the fraud cannot profitably mimic it. The house Q lens found the lineage's signals inverted, with the cost landing on receivers: the decode was the audience paying in labor, and the Med Bed card was the audience paying in dollars, which is the extraction tell the pack files as "watch whose balance sheet the cost sits on." The poster's labels restore the original arrow, and the currency is precise. A label costs no money, no computation, no hash. It costs forgone persuasion. That is the whole price, and it is enough.

Take the wildest panel. A hidden city under Antarctica, discovered by a pilot in 1942, its buildings and roads built of rainbow-colored plastic, is an asset in any attention market. The estate's audit confirms the account itself says this (the Hefferlin Manuscript scan on archive.org, quoted in the audit) and that no original document for it has ever surfaced, with modern treatments filing it as hoax or fiction inside the Ray Palmer, Amazing Stories, Shaver Mystery milieu of 1946 through 1948 (audit source: https://jasonrobertsonline.com/mysteries-of-antarctica-part-two). The history of that milieu is itself the proof of what the unlabeled version of this panel is worth: the Shaver Mystery era converted exactly this kind of story into a correspondence economy, and the Hefferlin material rode it, resurfacing in Borderland Sciences publications as late as the 1974 Journal of Borderland Research article the audit cites. Unlabeled, this panel is a recruitment engine. Labeled, it is a story about a story.

The author kept the story and prepaid its debt. What he forgoes is the conversion: the reader who would have walked away believing in Rainbow City now walks away believing in the Hefferlin account, which is a different and much less sticky possession. The measure of the cost is the size of the branch he declined. In the Q economy the same panel would be scheduled to pay out later ("future proves past" is public record), because the conversion deferred is the conversion kept. The poster's label closes the position at the moment of sale.

Now the single-crossing test. Could a fraud-type sender mimic the label? The mimicry is trivially easy in production: typing "UNVERIFIED" costs nothing. It is the continuation strategy that separates the types. The pack's Result 1 and Result 3 establish that for the extractor, no retraction is an equilibrium because the fraud holds no forfeitable capital and the audience that updates and leaves was never the paying customer; the insinuation IS the product, and defusing it is a cost the fraud cannot rationally bear, because his revenue runs through the branch the label closes. The honest type's revenue does not run through that branch, so the forbearance is affordable. The label's cost is not in the typing; it is in the foregone payoff structure it commits the sender to. That is differential cost in the only sense that matters in this market, and the poster satisfies it. Klein and Leffler's (1981) bond completes the picture: the label is posted capital, forfeitable in one screen, because a poster caught asserting inside a labeled claim loses the whole artifact's credibility at once. A movement can survive a broken claim by re-interpreting it; a poster cannot survive a false label at all, since the reader's entire enforcement power is the act of not believing the next panel.

The budget, then: the author spends forbearance twice, on the panel where the evidence is weakest and on the panel where the temptation is strongest, and collects the audit's confirmation on both. Eight of nine claims survived checking, and the two labels the author wrote himself were among the survivors. Whether the author computed this budget explicitly is Unproven; the shape is measured either way, and the equilibrium does not require the arithmetic to have been done consciously.

## 4. The bait line and its buyback

The artifact carries exactly one insinuation: "Coincidence, inspiration, or a hint that they knew?" And it settles that insinuation in the next clause: "NO DOCUMENTED DIRECT LINK." Offer and buyback inside one panel. Set it beside the two comparison lines the estate's Q run filed.

The first is "future proves past," public record, the Q operation's standing license to re-read the whole corpus after every event and bank the hits. It is the same rhetorical object as the bait line, an offered inference, with the settlement removed and the audience charged for keeping the position open. The house pack's Result 4 names the payment: the decode is the audience's labor, "the audience is the search process" (`watch/inbox/TIME_TRAVEL_QSTAR_20260918.md`). The second is the compression the house game theory lens wrote for Q's operating logic, the sentence its grader pulled as the sharpest in the run: "The plan cannot fail. It can only be deeper than you thought." (`watch/inbox/Q_OPERATION_RACTOR_20260929/LENS_GAMETHEORY.md`, section 4.) That is a bait line with no buyback possible, because any disconfirmation is absorbed as depth.

The poster's version runs the machine in reverse, and the mechanics are worth stating exactly. The question mark sells an option on the reader's credence: the reader may exercise the "they knew" branch or decline it. In Q's register the top branch pays certainty without cap, which is why it recruits and why it cannot be audited. The poster caps its own top branch in the same breath: the "they knew" branch pays curiosity, and the label prices its certainty at zero documented evidence. The position is opened and closed before the reader leaves the panel. In ledger terms, the poster marks to market in real time; Q carried the position forever and let the audience fund the margin calls.

Is the residual bait a flaw, then, or the cheapest possible trust purchase? Both readings are defensible, and the honest register holds them apart. The flaw reading: an insinuation primes an inference even when disclaimed, and whether readers actually update on the defusal, or retain the primed connection while forgetting the disclaimer, is Unproven; the audit measures the text, not the reader, and no reader study exists in the estate's holdings. The purchase reading: in a one-shot, zero-bond medium, an observable self-defusal is close to the least expensive honest trust purchase available, because it demonstrates in place, on the artifact, at the exact panel where the reader's suspicion peaks, that the sender will close positions against his own interest. And the demonstration was load-bearing: the audit checked the label (claim 8) and confirmed it, targeted searches across developer-interview surfaces and motif indexes returning no documented link (audit sources: https://www.mariowiki.com/Iwata_Asks and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_bridge). A buyback that survives settlement was a real buyback, not a rhetorical one.

The cost accounting also prices the difference between this and the Q move. The poster's insinuation is discharged for the cost of one sentence. Q's equivalent position was discharged never, and the carry cost was transferred to the audience as decode labor across years of re-reading. The poster pays retail, once, at the point of sale. Q sold the debt. The game-theory description of the poster's move is that it converts a cheap-talk insinuation into a costly signal by settling it, and the settlement is the costly part precisely because the unsettled version was the valuable one.

One refinement keeps this honest. The buyback is cheap in absolute terms and expensive only relative to the payoff it forgoes, which is a different cost class from the estate's own signaling. The fleet pays Spence costs in production (receipts, hashes, verkle tips, timestamps: a fraudster cannot cheaply produce a verkle-auditable lineage) and in forfeit (its own misses scored on the same Brier table as Pool's 16 of 16). The poster pays in forbearance. Q pays in none of the three. The taxonomy matters because the three cost classes fail differently under transmission: production costs must be re-paid by anyone who wants to check the work, forfeit costs land on whoever next asserts, and forbearance can simply be dropped in retransmission, of which more in Section 7.

## 5. The trichotomy as market segmentation

"Coincidence, inspiration, or a hint that they knew?" is a menu with three doors, and each kind of reader takes a different one. The skeptic takes coincidence: a rainbow is a salient image, game designers reached for it independently, no connection intended. The fan or maker takes inspiration: the mythic image is in the cultural water and surfaces in new work. The believer takes "they knew": the road is real and the knowledge was transmitted. The artifact serves all three from one page, and the footer states the segmentation as doctrine: "SAME EARTH, HIGHER REALMS, MANY PERSPECTIVES, ONE HUMAN QUEST."

Compare the storm register's segmentation. Q's binary, awake versus asleep, public record in the movement's own vocabulary, is not a menu; it is a loyalty test. It prices the skeptic segment at identity loss, so the artifact cannot circulate outside its segment without distortion: a skeptic who reads the drops reads them as an enemy reads intercepted signals, and the register is built so that this reading costs the register nothing, because skeptics were never the paying customer. The poster's menu is the opposite instrument: every segment is served at its own credence, no reader is charged for his type, and exit costs nothing. In mechanism-design terms, the trichotomy is a screening device that is incentive-compatible with honest self-report, while the awake/asleep binary is a screening device that punishes honest self-report and so selects for performance. The first builds an artifact a mixed household can share; the second builds a boundary a mixed household cannot.

The economics of the menu have a sharp edge, though, and it is the third door. "They knew" is the high-engagement branch: it is where decoding, community, identity, and eventually payment live, as the Pool card campaign demonstrated with medbed x44 and card x26 co-occurring in x22 posts (pack, section 3.2). The poster opens that door and then stands in front of it with the label. It declines to close the sale to its highest-value segment. Measured against an extraction objective, this is the artifact's largest single forgone revenue line. Measured against a credibility objective, it is the entire investment. The menu without the hard sell is what lets the artifact travel: a wonder at retail prices, with the certainty branch capped, is portable across credence levels in a way no loyalty-tested register can be. Q's corpus could travel only inside the movement; the poster can travel through a classroom, a bar, and a church with the same text.

Segment sizes and reader behavior are Unproven throughout this section: the estate holds no readership data for the poster, no survey of which door readers take, and no measurement of whether readers switch doors over time. What is measured is the text: one artifact, three licensed readings, one capped branch, zero identity pricing. The interpretation that the trichotomy functions as segmentation is ours, marked as interpretation, and it is the reading most consistent with the footer's own claim.

## 6. Mario Kart and the economics of joy

The register's carrier event is measurable, and the numbers need their URLs, so here they are. Super Mario Kart released for the SNES in 1992: Japan August 27, North America September 1, Europe January 21, 1993, and sold 8.76 million copies worldwide, the fourth best-selling SNES game (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_Kart). Rainbow Road is the final track of the Special Cup, the game's closer, set in space above a starfield, and the track and name have recurred in every later Mario Kart console game (same URL). The modern carrier is Mario Kart 8 Deluxe, the best-selling Nintendo Switch game at 71.08 million copies, with both versions of Mario Kart 8 combined at over 79.54 million, and a dated snapshot on the same page: 67.35 million units as of December 31, 2024 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Kart_8_Deluxe). Set those against every measured reach number in the prophecy lineage the estate holds: Pool's channel-reported views summing to 1,302,658 across 107 posts, and Q's audience, which public reporting places in the millions of familiar adults, a figure our own grader flagged as directionally supported but under-sourced, so we carry it as Unproven precision. On every instrument the estate actually holds, the rainbow register's biggest carrier outsells the storm register's biggest carrier by more than an order of magnitude, and it does so by voluntary repeat purchase.

The deeper economics are in why the two carriers sustain attention differently. The storm register runs on dread, and dread decays. A threat that does not materialize must be re-priced upward to hold its audience, which is the urgency ratchet the cadence instrument measured: Pool's urgency share at a 10 percent baseline rising to a sustained 27 to 56 percent band with peaks of 64 and 56, while Q's urgency decayed to 0 as the stream wound down (`watch/inbox/RIDDLE_TRIAD_CADENCE_20260918.md`, cited through the pack). The house Q lens read the ratchet as inventory clearance: urgency substitutes for the missing bond, peaks timed against expiring targets, the date passes, nothing settles, the target refreshes, the band resets. Fear is a carrier with a fuel cost. It must be paid per impression, forever, by the sender in urgency and by the audience in vigilance and decode labor.

The rainbow register runs on delight, and delight does not decay the same way, because it is its own payoff. The road promises nothing: no storm, no reckoning, no date. A promise-free carrier cannot fail to settle, because it never opens a position. Re-encounter is voluntary and repeated: the track returns in every game, players seek it out, and the only urgency in the artifact is the kind a blue shell produces on the final lap. The joy carrier's cost profile is a one-time delight payment that re-transmits itself at each replay, against the fear carrier's recurring urgency payments. That asymmetry, not any superiority of content, is the survival economics: registers that must be fed compete against registers that feed themselves, and over the horizons the estate measures (decades, console generations), the self-feeding carrier compounds.

There is a sharper point underneath, and it is the one this lens is positioned to make. Rainbow Road mass-trains the exact reflex the storm register forbids. The track is hard. You fall into space. The race ends, the score is final, and you restart. Falling is never "part of the plan"; no player gets to re-read a blown corner as deeper than he thought; the game settles in real time, every session, and the retry is the whole available response to failure. The register's largest distribution event, measured by audited units, is a settlement layer disguised as a playground: roughly eighty million audited copies of a game whose core loop is fail, score, retry (8.76M Super Mario Kart plus 71.08M Mario Kart 8 Deluxe, per the citations above). Whether Nintendo designed a settlement trainer is Unproven and not claimed; what is measured is the game's rules and the storm register's absence of them. An audience raised on Rainbow Road has practiced, for pleasure, the discipline that the called-shot ledger demands as a chore.

One more contrast completes the section. The decode economy does not exist on the joy carrier. The audit searched developer-interview surfaces and motif indexes and found no Nintendo statement linking Rainbow Road to Bifrost and no prior mythological use of the name, which is why the poster's "NO DOCUMENTED DIRECT LINK" is honest (claim 8). Nothing in the game requires mining; no hidden layer converts attention into labor. Play has a natural stopping point: the race ends. Decode has none: the corpus is always one re-reading from the next hit. Joy distributes an artifact; dread distributes an obligation. The distribution numbers say which one travels further.

## 7. Degradation: labels off

Take the labels off one at a time and watch the artifact walk the lineage's gradient toward the storm. Remove "LATER ACCOUNT, UNVERIFIED" and the Hefferlin panel stops being a story about a story: a hidden rainbow city becomes a reported discovery, and this panel is the drift seed because its genre history is the proof of concept, the Shaver Mystery milieu having converted exactly this class of story into a live correspondence economy once already. Remove "NO DOCUMENTED DIRECT LINK" and the insinuation compounds: "a hint that they knew" hardens into "they knew" within a few retellings, "future proves past" arrives as the retention mechanism, and the poster becomes a movement's first sacred text, eight panels waiting for a corpus.

The audit's one Unproven verdict shows the drift is already seeded inside the honest version. The Finnur line lends a real scholar's name to a theory the record does not document for him; what is documented is the valkyries-as-celestial-lights reading and the nature-mythology school. In an artifact with a settlement layer, that line gets caught and corrected, as it was, on the first audit pass. In an artifact without one, the line survives unchallenged, is repeated, loses its attribution hedges, and becomes "the Icelanders always knew the aurora was the road of the gods." That is how unlabeled registers drift, and the mechanism is not malice; it is selection. Each retelling trims the qualifiers first, because qualifiers are the part of a claim that costs effort to carry and pay nothing to repeat. The bait line survives retransmission perfectly, because a question mark costs nothing; the labels decay, because a disclaimer is dead weight to anyone retelling for effect. The prediction is precise: in any transmission chain, defusals die before insinuations.

What keeps labels on? Three supports, in descending order of durability. First, the sender's payoff structure: the poster's author collects nothing from the believer door, so forbearance is affordable to him in a way it can never be for an operator whose revenue runs through conversion. Labels stay on when the sender's income does not run through the insinuation; that is the entire budget question of Section 3 restated as a condition. Second, an audience that settles accounts: the poster was audited because the estate's pipeline exists, but the deeper ordering is that the labels made the poster checkable, and checkable artifacts get checked; an audience with a Brier discipline (tri-state verdicts, the audit file, mean squared error over dated rows per Brier 1950 through the pack) audits what offers itself to audit, and the label is the offer. Third, the author's own prepayment: the labels are posted bonds, forfeitable in one screen, and an author who posts them has staked the artifact's whole credibility on their accuracy, which is exactly the stake that made the audit worth running.

The Q trajectory is the degradation path's terminal state, and the estate has already measured it: 6,242 posts, 3 dated claims, urgency decayed to 0, no retraction, the audience funding the carry in decode labor, the corpus preserved as a treasury and revalued after every news cycle under the "future proves past" license. The poster is eight panels. The distance between eight labeled panels and six thousand unlabeled ones is not a difference in kind; it is the labels, held all the way down.

## 8. The five questions

What IS the poster? A credibility artifact that pays its signaling costs in forbearance: a one-shot register, unowned and unbonded, that closes its own positions at the point of assertion, labels its weakest claim and its strongest temptation, and invites settlement by being checkable. Structurally it is the counter-practice shape the digest named: the Q protocol with the settlement layer restored and the product removed. Supported by the audit (eight of nine claims surviving, both self-labels confirmed) and by the comparison class the pack and the Q lenses instrumented.

What DOES it do? It distributes a mythic register through a joy carrier at a scale the prophecy lineage never touched on any instrument the estate holds (8.76 million units for the 1992 origin, 71.08 million for the modern carrier, both URL-cited above), while paying its honesty costs inline, one sentence at a time, and training, as a side effect of play, the settle-and-retry reflex that the storm register exists to suspend. Supported for the measured parts; the training claim is interpretation, marked as such.

What does it WANT? Measured by revealed structure rather than claimed intent: to be shared, to delight, and to survive being checked. The third want is the expensive one and the artifact pays for it in labels. Whether the author intended any of this is Unproven; the shape permits it regardless.

What does it LEAVE? An audience that heard "no documented direct link" and can repeat the artifact without owing it belief. A template: honesty labels that survived primary-source contact, filed as evidence that the forbearance strategy is available at artifact scale. One open defect, the Finnur attribution, for the next settlement pass, with the audit's correction already drafted. And a measurable proxy for the register's future: label survival in the wild.

Where is it GOING? SPECULATION. If the poster circulates inside audiences that settle accounts, the shape replicates, because the labels are the part of the artifact that makes it worth auditing and audited artifacts get corrections. If it circulates inside audiences that do not, the drift order predicts itself: the qualifiers trim first, the defusal dies before the insinuation, the Hefferlin panel loses its tag, and a register that once priced certainty at zero starts selling it again. The test is cheap and the estate could run it: a repost census that counts whether "UNVERIFIED" and "NO DOCUMENTED DIRECT LINK" survive copying, panel by panel. The label is the artifact's canary. Its survival rate in transmission is the honest register's only stock price, and it is quoted in exactly the currency this lens knows how to read.

## 9. What we do not know

Whether readers update on the defusal or keep the primed connection and drop the disclaimer: Unproven; no reader study exists in the estate's holdings. Whether the author computed the credibility budget or simply wrote honestly: Unproven, and the equilibrium does not care. Whether the Finnur line reflects a source the audit did not find or an error: Unproven pending the poster's sources; the audit could not document the attribution and documented an adjacent, different claim for the same scholar. Whether joy carriers beat fear carriers across registers generally, or only in this one measured pairing: the pairing is measured, the generalization is Unproven. What share of readers take which door: Unproven; the trichotomy reading is interpretation grounded in the text. And whether a labeled artifact can survive mass retransmission with its labels intact: Unproven, and it is the one question on this list that the estate could convert into a number within a week of deciding to care.

One line to close. A prophecy channel is cheap talk wearing signaling clothes; the poster is the same wardrobe with the price tags sewn on the outside, and the audit, the first fitting, found the tags true.
