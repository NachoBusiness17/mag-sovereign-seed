## 1. FABRICATION CHECK

The pack is cleaner than the hostile grader expected. No essay fabricated a quotation. Every
primary-quote spot-check that could be run came back verbatim or was flagged by the essay
itself. The defects found are of four kinds: a detail imported past a cited source, two
chapter mis-attributions, a handful of quote-fidelity compressions, and several numbers that
do not reconcile with the essay's own citations. Each is listed by essay and section.

### LENS_EAST

**E1 (Section IV, the pack's worst defect).** "Fazang, the third patriarch, demonstrated the
doctrine for Empress Wu with a golden statue ringed by mirrors," and later, "Fazang needed
eight mirrors and a Buddha statue to make courtiers see it." The cited source (Wikipedia,
Indra's net) says Fazang "used the golden statue of a lion" for Empress Wu. No mirrors, no
count of eight, no courtiers appear in that article. The hall-of-mirrors story exists in other
scholarship, but this essay cites exactly one reference and then closes the section with
"Tri-state: all textual claims in this section are Supported through the reference above."
That declaration is false for two sentences. A concrete ritual detail and a concrete number
were imported from outside the cited source under a blanket Supported stamp, which is the
exact failure mode this pack exists to catch.

**E2 (Section II).** Hayashi Ōen is quoted as holding the bridge is "invisible and intangible
except to those purified." The source reads "ordinarily both invisible and intangible to those
who had not purified their hearts of foreign cultural influence." The quoted span is a
rewording, not a quotation, and the compression drops the nativist qualifier that gives the
claim its content. A reader of the essay would not learn that Ōen's purity condition was
specifically anti-foreign.

**E3 (Section II, minor).** "Makes the gods 'laugh heartily'" shifts the source's "laughed
heartily at the sight" into present tense inside quotation marks. Trivial, but this is a pack
about load-bearing quotes.

**E4 (Section I and footnote 3).** The red-is-fire line, "That which thou seest to be red in
the bow is burning fire," is attributed to Gylfaginning chapter 13. In Brodeur, the edition
both the audit and the essay say they verified against, that line is in chapter 15 (it sits
between Brodeur's XV and XVI headings; the bridge passage it belongs to narratively is
chapter 13, but the Q and A that produces the line is chapter 15). The error is inherited from
the audit's "same chapter" phrasing, but East repeats it as chapter 13 in its own footnote
after the audit had been corrected in every other respect. See W2 below: the West essay made
the mirrored error in the other direction.

**E5 (Section I).** "It theorizes the bridge (aurora), maps the bridge (1824 to 1982), builds
the bridge..." repeats the digest's wave framing without carrying the audit's central
correction, that the Finnur Magnússon aurora identification is Unproven. The West and game
theory essays carry that correction prominently; East, which read the same audit, launders the
ghost through a contrast paragraph. Mild, because the sentence names no theorist, but the
asymmetry is visible.

**E6 (Section VI, GOING, SPECULATION).** "A billion players have crossed a rainbow road" and
"a million hands on a wheel" are invented magnitudes with no derivation. The audit declined,
correctly, to attach a number to "global phenomenon," and this section reattaches one anyway.
The SPECULATION marking makes it lawful, not correct.

**E7 (minor).** The Van Norden quotation is slightly reordered inside the quote marks ("every
other thing that exists" versus the source's "every other thing"); "Khenpo A-chö" and "Khenpo
A-chos" drift between text and footnote 14; the "third-century" dating of the Buddhāvataṃsaka
is the cited article's own loose phrasing, carried faithfully, so it is inherited looseness
rather than fabrication.

**Verified clean for East, stated so the grade is fair:** every Kojiki quotation verbatim,
including the footnote about "the best authorities" being at variance, and the essay's note
that the digitized text prints "stiffed" for "stirred" is byte-accurate against the live page;

## 4. TENSION MAP

The three lenses collide on the surface and hold together underneath. State the collisions as
they actually stand, then resolve each.

**The surface collision.** East says the true road is internal: the register's completion is
the traveler becoming the road, and the footer already preaches it. West says the register's
actual career is externalization: image to theory to map to city to physics, each wave
denying it is handling myth. Game theory says the poster is a credibility instrument: labels
as posted bonds, the one insinuation prepaid, forbearance as the signaling cost. Read
shallowly, East makes the poster a vessel of the deepest answer, West makes it the latest
reification, and game theory makes it a market position. Three different objects.

**The unified picture: the register is a conversion, and the three lenses are its three
faces.** The rainbow register is not the image (a colored arc) and not the structures
(bridge, treatise, city, wormhole, racetrack). It is the operation that converts the image
into whatever the receiving age can hold. The East converts the road into the traveler:
doctrine, practice, and at the limit a body of light, the final material. The West converts
the road into the world: theory, map, municipality, spacetime, infrastructure, the final
material. Game theory prices the conversion's honesty overhead: every conversion event is an
assertion, assertions open positions, and the label is the position closed at the point of
assertion. All three descriptions hold of one register because all three describe one verb at
different zoom levels, and the poster is the one artifact in its own sequence that performs
all three at once: the panels run the West's conversion (image to map to city to track), the
footer runs the East's (the road withdrawn into the traveler), and the two honest labels run
game theory's (the conversion's cost paid inline, in forbearance, at the exact panels where
the reader's suspicion peaks). The register converts in both directions at once, outward into
structure and inward into self, and the credibility surface is the seam where the two
directions meet the reader. One register; three lenses; one verb.

**Resolution one: joy-carrier versus fear-carrier.** The collision is East's completion versus
game theory's compounding versus West's maintenance instinct. Resolution: fear is a carried
position, joy is a prepaid one. The storm register opens a position with every drop and never
settles it; urgency is paid per impression by the sender and vigilance and decode labor by the
receivers, forever, which is the West's maintenance clause applied to attention. The rainbow
road promises nothing, so it never opens a position, so it never needs a buyback, so replay is
free and retransmission compounds. The West lens explains why joy has no carry cost: the
register's own instinct is to strip maintenance clauses, and joy is the one carrier with no
maintenance clause to strip; the red fire that keeps giants off has no analogue in a track
whose only hazard is falling into a starfield. The East lens explains why joy wins at depth:
the storm register withholds completion (the plan is always deeper, the crossing never
finishes), while the rainbow road completes every lap; the game theory finding that
self-feeding carriers compound is the measurable face of the Eastern claim that a road must
be become rather than awaited. Joy outcompetes fear for the same reason the rainbow body
finishes and the storm never does: completion is the payoff, and only one register sells it.

**Resolution two: the demolition clause versus built permanence.** Ragnarök is the one
maintenance clause the register ever wrote honestly: a guard, a fire band, and a date of
failure, and every Western wave since is a refusal of that date. Nature-mythology makes the
bridge a recurring celestial fact; the map fixes it in ink; the city grounds it in Antarctic
plastic; the film turns it into spacetime structure that does not break; the racetrack
rebuilds it fresh every race. The East resolves the collision directly: the only structure
that survives the demolition date is the one that stopped being a structure. The rainbow body
is the bridge's end-of-life executed without loss; Bifröst's is executed under enemy cavalry;
the race ends, the score is final, and the player presses restart. The register rehearses
ending in three keys, as catastrophe (build, and the span breaks under you), as graduation
(become, and the road dissolves into light), and as play (race, and the lap ends and costs
nothing). Every wave builds permanence onto a document that expires it because the register
is trying, wave by wave, to convert its own scheduled death from a failure into something
cheaper: first a theory, then an image, then a game.

**Resolution three: what the register WANTS if the East is right about the footer.** It wants
to be finished. If the footer is the register's own doctrine arriving through the side door,
then the register's want is not worship of the image and not faith in the crossing but the
completion of the traveler, which is the one good all three lenses locate: the West wants a
crossing that does not require faith, the East wants a faith that does not require a
crossing, and these are the same want priced in different currencies. The footer is where the
poster quotes both rates at once, and it is the only panel that pays out regardless of the
reader's credence: "follow the light within" costs a skeptic nothing to decline, a fan nothing
to admire, a believer nothing to keep, which is exactly the label shape the game theorist
found everywhere else in the artifact. The register's want, unified: settlement without debt.
It wants its claims checkable (the labels), its structures walkable (the physics), and its
walkers to be the road (the dissolution); the footer is the one line on the poster where all
three wants are already satisfied, which is why it is small, at the bottom, and easy to
overlook. The register wants its own completion, and it printed the completion where completion
usually goes: in the fine print.

## 5. VERDICT

**Strongest essay as written: LENS_WEST.** It survived hostile re-verification with the
fewest and mildest wounds (two chapter mislabels riding on verbatim quotes, one archive-month
assertion, one off-by-one line range), it executed the pack's hardest precision task (carrying
the Finnur attribution as Unproven while keeping the documented reading) without a wobble, it
is the only essay whose hardest quotations are byte-verified against the estate's own shelf,
and its conversion frame is the structure the other two essays quietly stand on. Game theory
is the most intellectually portable and has the best mechanism; East is the best written and
has the best single idea. West is the best made object.

**What each lacks.**

East lacks a counter-test. "Follow the light within" is at least as Western devotional and
Platonic as Eastern; the essay asserts the footer as Eastern doctrine arriving through the
side door and never tests the rival hypothesis that it is greeting-card boilerplate with an
independent Western pedigree, which would cost the thesis its central convergence claim. It
also needs the Fazang sentence repaired and the eight mirrors cut or sourced.

West lacks an account of the image itself. It is a complete history of receptions with no
theory of why a colored arc reads as a road in the first place; the essay explains eight
centuries of building and nothing about the initial pull that kept the crews employed. It
needs its chapter numbers repaired, and it hands Mario Kart away one section before its own
audience arrives at scale.

Game theory lacks arithmetic hygiene. Its own numbers contradict its own citations inside one
section, its denominators float, and its verdict count does not match the instrument it is
quoting. It also lacks any observation of readers (flagged, honestly, but section 5 still runs
three paragraphs on the assumption) and an answer to the sender-identity problem: the
equilibrium argument requires knowing what the author's revenue runs through, and the author
is a name on a poster.

**The single sharpest sentence across all three:** "Four maps in sequence feel like four
witnesses. They are one witness with four hands." (LENS_WEST, Section I.) It is the sharpest
because it does the most work against the most resistance: it takes the poster's central
evidentiary rhetoric, the four-map row, and converts it into a measurable defect in fourteen
words, while leaving the poster's honesty intact, since a drawing tradition is real
transmission and only the evidentiary gloss is refused. Runners-up: "The road you become is a
road you cannot fall from" (East, Section VII), and "In any transmission chain, defusals die
before insinuations" (game theory, Section 7), the one sentence in the pack that a stranger
could falsify with a clipboard and a week.
